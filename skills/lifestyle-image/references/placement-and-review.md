# Placement and visual review

## Read the two references separately

Product evidence controls design and finish. Environment evidence controls the setting. Another faucet visible in the room may identify the replacement site, but it must not donate its handles, curvature, proportions or finish to the target product. An environment moodboard with multiple rooms does not identify a unique base scene; ask which panel should anchor the image if the choice affects the result.

A lifestyle photograph should make the product convincing in a real context without outsourcing room design to a generic prompt. Record a few concrete environmental anchors: cabinet arrangement and style, counter/backsplash materials and pattern, sink/cooking-area location, wall/floor colour, daylight direction and camera height. Use what is actually visible. Do not embellish an inexpensive reference room into a new marble mansion merely because the audience buys premium products.

For explicitly requested inspiration mode, distinguish the features to carry over from adaptations the user actually requested. A closer crop may be straightforward; a reverse camera angle can require unseen room and product geometry. Do not treat permission to recompose the room as permission to invent the product.

## Physical placement

- **Wall pot filler:** retain wall mounting, flange/valves, exact arm count, joints and folded/unfolded configuration visible in the product reference. Use a plausible backsplash position above the cooking area, with credible reach and clearance. Do not add a free-floating deck leg or change arm lengths to reach a pot. Do not invent exact compliant clearance from a photograph.
- **Deck faucet/bridge faucet:** align mounting centres with the sink/counter plane, place the spout over the basin and retain handle access. Keep actual asymmetry, separate risers and plates. An existing single-hole opening does not establish a compatible two-hole installation; resolve a known conflict rather than concealing it.
- **Wall faucet/shower hardware:** respect wall plane, mounting plates, component spacing, hose path and plausible shower/sink zone. Do not scale components independently to fit tiles. Do not move unrelated room fixtures just to make space unless requested.
- **Other products:** infer an appropriate support, hanging or mounting relationship from the references, and retain the product's real functional orientation.

Use measured dimensions when supplied. Familiar room objects can suggest relative scale but are not a calibrated ruler. State approximate scale when measurements are absent; ask for product/site dimensions if accurate fit is the purpose of the image. If the angle mismatch would expose unknown geometry, a supported alternate view is better than bending the photographed object.

## Photographic integration

Match camera perspective, contact/attachment and depth of field. The environment is the lighting authority: adapt the product to the room's light direction, colour temperature, exposure, contrast, local shadow softness and nearby reflection environment. Preserve the room's existing illumination rather than globally relighting it to fit the catalogue photo. Keep every small physical product detail intact while changing its apparent lighting response. Avoid a pasted-on white studio halo, double shadows, mirror reflections that still show the removed fixture, and reflected props not present in the scene.

Separate material identity from lighting response. Environmental colour may appear naturally in highlights/reflections, but brass base colour must not be converted to orange copper or generic saturated gold. Do not preserve an incompatible studio highlight merely to keep the source pixel brightness unchanged. Preserve antique patina/brushed texture where present; a polished studio anchor is not a licence to polish every finish. Handle dark rubber/ceramic/glass independently. Do not recolour the entire kitchen to make the insert look consistent.

## Review gates

Inspect the whole scene and a native-size product crop against the inputs:

| Gate | Look for |
| --- | --- |
| Product identity | Same silhouette and ALL details: component count, handle asymmetry, curves/thickness, joints, seams, screw heads, ring/collar and edge profiles, holes, outlet/aerator, plates, engraving and surface texture |
| Finish | Source-consistent hue/undertone, patina, gloss and texture; no warmer gold conversion or plastic appearance |
| Dimensions and proportions | Mandatory [post-generation audit](dimensional-audit.md): original world height/reach supported by camera/site evidence; matching-view straight-riser, arch, body, tube and handle ratios; unsupported measurements are unresolved |
| Installation | Correct mounting surface, support, contact, orientation, clearances and credible relative scale |
| Integration | Environment-led light direction/temperature/exposure/contrast and highlight/shadow softness; matched perspective, occlusion and reflections; no conflicting studio highlights, halos or floating edges |
| Environment | Recognizable reference layout/materials/palette and requested viewpoint; no unrelated room invention |
| Scope | No unrequested props, text, duplicates, accessories, missing parts or room restyling |

A known tiny product-detail change fails the image just as a major silhouette error does. When one gate fails, name the specific defect and target the correction. Keep both original input groups. A visually plausible result is not proof of pixel-identical product geometry or installation fit. Stop with a concrete unresolved status after two unsuccessful corrections; do not hide that status in a positive summary.


## Full-size fit and camera record

Before generation record: (1) authoritative product measurements and what each measures; (2) site/basin width/depth and deck clearance with source or explicit estimated range; (3) outlet landing point using original reach and handle/overhead clearance; (4) exact source product view and agreement with room camera elevation, mounting plane, spout direction and handle-axis depth. Reject incompatible or unsupported pairings before the edit call. Use a well-supported room rather than forcing an attractive one.

Review world-size consistency after generation against the same dimensional anchors, allowing perspective projection but no arbitrary world-size reduction. If the fixture looks too large for the basin, choose another environment; do not “correct” by shrinking the whole fixture or altering any component. If an output violates the dimensional plan, regenerate to the original plan without moving the goalposts. If that repeatedly fails, mark rejected. Review exact details at native size; a tiny fixture in a wide shot is insufficient evidence to claim microdetail fidelity.
