# Find three real environment photographs

Read this when the user provides product evidence but no environment photograph. Do not generate an environment as an intermediate shortcut. The final composites are generated edits, but their environment bases must be actual real-world photographs.

## Search and provenance

1. Identify the product's real installation type, supported viewing angles and likely setting from its photos/gallery: for example a wall pot filler above a cooking area, a deck faucet by a sink, or a wall light at a compatible wall position. Keep the exact product/variant fixed.
2. Search the web/image search for appropriate real installed spaces. Prefer original interior-designer project portfolios, architectural photographers, documented renovations, or manufacturer installation case studies with traceable photography. Seek three distinct spaces with useful composition and sufficient resolution, rather than three crops of one photo.
3. Open the source page and inspect the actual image. Record the project/site, source page URL, image URL and photographer/credit or other evidence that it depicts a photographed built space. An image-search thumbnail, Pinterest repost or AI-detector score by itself does not establish provenance.
4. Exclude content labelled AI-generated, render, visualization, concept render or virtual staging, and sources centred on synthetic inspiration. Also reject materially uncertain provenance; do not claim a photograph is verified real solely because it looks realistic. Use positive project/photography evidence, not just the absence of an AI label. If no source can establish enough real photographs, report the shortfall rather than fabricating certainty or silently using AI rooms.
5. Check access to the usable source image before choosing it. Do not select an attractive but inaccessible thumbnail as though a usable environment has been obtained. Retain source links for attribution and later retrieval; do not imply that source discovery establishes publication rights.

## Suitability before generation

For each candidate, assess:
- A credible installation surface/location for this actual product, and space for its full dimensions/configuration.
- A camera angle supported by available exact-product photos, including mounting-plane orientation and visible spindle/body depth.
- Original product dimensions with exact measurement definitions, plus site/basin width and depth, deck/mounting space, reach landing point, handle clearance and overhead space. Use source specifications or identifiable dimensional anchors, label estimates and reject a known small-site mismatch. No arbitrary physical shrinking/enlargement is permitted.
- Lighting that can be matched without erasing source material identity or fine product details.
- Adequate resolution and visibility, without foreground clutter hiding the installation or forcing invented product parts.

Record a pass/fail decision for full-size fit and camera compatibility BEFORE choosing the three bases. For a failed pair, find another room; do not prompt the generator to scale the product down, widen the sink, change the spout direction or invent a missing product view. An “approximate” label does not turn a visibly implausible installation into an acceptable image.

Prefer achievable geometry and clear inspection over dramatic lighting or a fashionable room. Three distinctive styles are useful when all are compatible; do not force style variety at the expense of the product.

## Use and review

By default, choose the three verified bases and proceed directly to one edit per environment, each with the same product references and its own environment-led lighting. Do not stop at a proposal stage unless the user asks to select environments first. Keep each room grounded in its source photograph; do not replace it with a generic AI interpretation.

For every slot record provenance and the source image actually passed to the image tool. Review product details and camera/lighting integration independently. Mark a known wrong detail or implausible installation rejected; never hide it because the other two images failed. Deliver three usable options when feasible, and accurately report any rejected slot or unavailable source rather than promising guaranteed generative fidelity.
